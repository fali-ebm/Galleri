1 · Three arms

Kunzmann once · Galleri every year

Kunzmann is scored once at the start age; 35.7% score 8 or more (GRADE evidence profile, sensitivity 77.5%, specificity 64.4%) and get one EGD, then usual care. Galleri sensitivity is 63.7% in round 1 and ×0.775 after. Every end state feeds the natural-history model (figure 5).

2 · Galleri +ve: the EGD step

hold · two kinds of miss

After a negative EGD, repeat positives skip EGD for 2 years (the shorter of the gastric 2.37-year and esophageal adenocarcinoma 4.9-year sojourn estimates) and go straight to ENT or PET CT. EGD finds 88.7% of cancers present (Menon 2014). Of the misses, 26% are cancers not yet visible (Kamran 2023 root causes A + C), which become visible after 1 year; the rest are visible lesions that were missed.

3 · After a negative or skipped EGD

ENT or PET CT

A missed visible upper GI cancer can be seen on PET CT (*0.5, a DUMMY placeholder) and is then found by a targeted EGD. Incidental findings are followed up; the benign ones count toward the false-positive burden.

4 · Pancreatobiliary finding, by history

tumor board · coin-toss sampling

First finding: EUS, with FNA/FNB a coin toss (beta(2,2)); a negative or unsampled EUS goes to tumor board, which sends 10% to diagnostic laparoscopy and the rest to watchful waiting. A repeat finding is a personalised decision: after a negative EUS, laparoscopy is half as likely (0.05) and repeat EUS 15%; after a negative laparoscopy, laparoscopy is a tenth as likely (0.01) and repeat EUS 6%. The history is permanent.

Repeat positives after a negative workup

Each person has their own false-positive propensity, drawn from beta(mκ, (1−m)κ) with mean m = 0.036% per test. After k earlier false positives in t − 1 tests, the chance the next test is falsely positive is (mκ + k) / (κ + t − 1). The base case uses κ = 1,000,000 (tests independent); the scenarios κ = 3,400 and 2,900 make a second false positive 1.8× and 1.96× as likely. The cohort model carries tags for 0, 1 and 2+ earlier false positives; the microsimulation draws each person's propensity.

5 · Natural history

same in every arm

Onset is solved so that, without screening, symptomatic diagnoses reproduce SEER incidence by age, sex and race/ethnicity, and their stage reproduces SEER's stage mix. Screen-detected cancers wait in lead-time states until they would have caused symptoms, so screening earns stage shift only. The cohort model steps once a year; the microsimulation runs the same hazards in continuous time (hesim).

6 · Esophageal adenocarcinoma calibrated to MISCAN-ESO

scenario · cohort model

In the MISCAN-ESO scenario, esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) follows the CISNET MISCAN-ESO structure: preclinical cancer moves from an early to a late stage before symptoms, with exponential durations. Mean sojourn 4.9 years in men and 4.5 in women (Kong 2014). The three rates are solved so that the sojourn, SEER's late-stage share at diagnosis (net of other-cause death) and the early/late dwell ratio are met exactly. A screen-detected EAC is early if found in the early phase, so its stage comes from the natural history. Gastric and other esophageal cancer keep the base-case natural history. Diagnosed EAC has esophagus-specific survival.

The microsimulation finds about 23% fewer cancers by screening because, in continuous time, some cancers start and cause symptoms between two annual tests; the cohort model's yearly steps give every cancer at least one test. That is why its ICER is higher.

What changed since the last version

  • EGD hold of 2 years after a negative EGD; repeat positives go straight to ENT or PET CT.
  • Two kinds of EGD miss: a visible lesion missed, and a cancer not yet visible.
  • Pancreatobiliary workup by history: coin-toss FNA/FNB, tumor board, laparoscopy less likely after each negative result.
  • Bayesian repeat positives (κ scenarios), and Kunzmann as a one-time test from the GRADE evidence profile.
  • Person-level microsimulation (hesim) that cross-checks the cohort model; it also corrected how workup paths are weighted for people with cancers outside the upper GI tract.
  • MISCAN-ESO calibration of esophageal adenocarcinoma (scenario): sex-specific sojourn, early → late preclinical progression, stage of screen-detected EAC from the natural history, esophagus-specific survival; validated against CISNET lifetime cases and deaths.
  • Reproducible in R only: the inputs workbook and all three pages are now built by R scripts; Rscript make_all.R reruns everything.

Still not modelled: stage varying with age within a race-sex group, the natural history of other-site cancers (they borrow upper GI's), and a hold that depends on a Bayesian probability threshold.
